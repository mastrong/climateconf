#9 Data: This time it’s personal

Author(s): Niko Kommenda, Daniel Wolfe

Speaker Biographies

Niko Kommenda

Niko Kommenda has worked as a graphics journalist in major UK and US newsrooms for around 10 years, most recently as climate graphics reporter at the Washington Post. He co-anchored the Post’s Climate Lab column, answering everyday questions around climate and the natural world with visuals and data.

Daniel Wolfe

Daniel Wolfe is a journalist using interactive graphics and novel data analysis to report on our changing environment and its impacts on public health. Previously he co-anchored a column at The Washington Post, The Climate Lab. He is a Pulitzer Center, Metcalf, and an NYU Stern Climate Economics Journalism Fellow.

Daniel Wolfe and Niko Kommenda, the former authors of the Washington Post’s data-driven Climate Lab column, discuss how interactive visualizations and personalized tools in particular help readers engage with scientific data.

The talk is aimed not only at other data and visual journalists but at researchers keen to extend their media reach. Throughout our time working on the Washington Post’s climate desk, we’ve often found that scientists produce vast amounts of unique data but are unsure how to go about sharing it with the wider public beyond a press release and perhaps some basic charts.

We’ll draw on our experiences and learnings to discuss the following questions:

* How do you personalize data with visualizations?

* What do we think our readers want from climate visuals? What worked, what didn't?

* What makes a piece of scientific research appealing or suitable for data visualization?

* Where might science communication be headed as scientists and journalists become increasingly data- and visually literate?
