#1 2D/3D visualization for ensemble forecasting of coastal submersion

Author(s): Julius Bañgate, Jacques Gautier, Sidonie Christophe, Déborah Idier, Denis Paradis, Alice Dalphinet, Sophie Lecacheux

Speaker Biographies

Julius M. BAÑGATE

PhD Computer Science from Université Grenoble Alpes, France, MSc in Remote Sensing and BSc Geodetic Engineering from the University of the Philippines-Diliman. Currently a Postdoctoral Researcher for the ANR-ORACLES Project at LASTIG, IGN, Geodata Paris, Université Gustave Eiffel, France. Works on 2D/3D geovisualization, multi-agent modelling and simulation of complex systems.

Jacques Gautier

Jacques Gautier is lecturer in geovisualization at LASTIG laboratory (Univ. Gustave Eiffel, IGN) and at Géodata Paris. His research topics covers spatio-temporal data and uncertainty visualization, with application in meteorological, urban climate, and urban mobility thematics. He is teaching visualization courses at Géodata Paris, ENPC, and AgroParisTech.

Simulation models, generating meteo-oceanic conditions and submerged areas scenarios, are a major asset for forecasting coastal floods and prepare at risk populations. Forecasting is still performed by human operators, using visualization interfaces. In order to take model uncertainty into account in the determination of alert levels, advances in computing allow to perform ensemble simulation, by generating a set a possible scenarios. The visual interpretation of ensemble simulation outputs however requires specific approaches of visualization, allowing human operators to determine trends, identify outliers, and assess uncertainty, from many simulated scenarios at different spatial scales. While geospatial ensemble visualization approaches mainly use 2D representation of aggregated indicators, 3D interfaces could be a way to depict more precisely the distribution of ensemble at different spatial locations. We present 2D and 2D+1 visualization approaches aiming at helping forecasters to visually analyze ensemble simulation of submerged coastal areas, at different spatial scales and for different levels of complexity.
