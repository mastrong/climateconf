#5 Turning Climate Data into Comics and Visual Narratives

Author(s): Inés Martín del Real, Andria Nicodemou, Antonella Mele, Emma D'Orto, Marta Terrado, Arianna Acierno

Speaker Biographies

Andria Nicodemou

Andria Nicodemou is a climate science communicator in the Knowledge Integration team of the Earth Sciences department at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center, translating complex climate concepts into accessible content within European projects.

Inés Martín del Real

Inés Martín del Real is a science communicator in the Knowledge Integration Team of the Earth Sciences Department at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center, working on European projects and exploring creative ways, including science comics, to bring climate science to wider audiences.

Emma d'Orto

Emma D'Orto is a Science Communication Specialist at the CMCC Foundation, working on climate change adaptation projects. Her background spans Physics, science communication, education, and filmmaking.

Antonella Mele

Antonella Mele is a Science Communication Specialist at the CMCC Foundation (since 2023), working on European climate adaptation projects. Her background spans Biology, Global Change Ecology, and science communication.

Communicating complex climate data and concepts, such as overshoot and uncertainty, remains a challenge when engaging non-expert audiences. Effective visual communication approaches can bring complex data to life, not just presenting it, but also driving the story. This makes information more accessible and intuitive for diverse audiences, enabling deeper understanding and more meaningful interaction.

In this workshop, we will examine how comics and other visual communication formats can complement traditional data visualisation approaches by embedding quantitative information within storytelling, and using visual metaphors and simplified graphical representations.

First, we will introduce data-driven storytelling formats like comics, infographics or interactive web stories, and explore with participants how underlying data (e.g. temperature overshoot, emission scenarios) are transformed into narrative elements. We will discuss with the audience best practices and processes for developing such visual formats, and for preserving the balance between engagement and scientific robustness.

Next, the workshop will focus on a hands-on group activity in which participants create a short comic (1-3 panels) based on provided climate data or concepts. This exercise will allow participants to experiment with translating data into visual narratives that retain meaning while increasing accessibility.

By the end of the session, participants will have gained practical experience in narrative data visualisation and a transferable method for communicating complex climate data.
